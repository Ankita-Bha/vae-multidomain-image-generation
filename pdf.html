1. Agenda — What This Project Does

Core Goal

This project builds a system that learns what handwritten digits and fashion items look like, then generates brand new images that never existed before — like an artist who studies thousands of paintings and then creates their own. The generated images are then tested to confirm a separate AI (a CNN classifier) can still recognise them correctly.

4. Key Terms and Abbreviations

VAE
Variational Autoencoder — AI that compresses images into a short "code" (latent space) and reconstructs them. The "variational" part means it learns a range of codes, enabling entirely new image generation.
GAN
Generative Adversarial Network — Two AIs in competition: a generator creates images; a discriminator spots fakes. Their rivalry forces progressively sharper, more realistic outputs.
CNN
Convolutional Neural Network — AI built for image understanding. Uses filters that scan across pixels to detect edges, shapes, and patterns. Used here as the classifier and evaluator.
KL Divergence
Kullback-Leibler Divergence — Measures difference between two probability distributions. In VAEs, keeps the latent space smooth and organised so new images can be reliably sampled.
BCE
Binary Cross-Entropy — Loss function used by the GAN discriminator to measure how wrong a real/fake prediction is. Lower BCE = discriminator is more accurate.
Recon Loss
Reconstruction Loss — How closely the VAE output matches the original input image. Like measuring: "how blurry or accurate is the copy?" Lower is better.
Latent Space
A compressed mathematical representation of an image. A 28×28 image (784 pixels) becomes just 32 numbers. Similar images cluster together in this space, making targeted generation possible.
Beta (β)
Controls the balance between visual clarity and latent organisation. High β = organised space, blurrier images. Low β (0.05 in Sharp VAE) = sharper images, less structured space.
Epoch
One complete pass through the entire training dataset. Like reading a textbook once. More epochs generally means better learned patterns.
Grad-CAM
Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping — Produces a heatmap showing which image regions the CNN focused on for its decision. Makes AI reasoning explainable to humans.
ResNet-18
Residual Network (18 layers) — A well-known CNN architecture. Modified here to accept 1-channel grayscale images. Used as the classifier and image quality judge.
Checkpoint
A saved copy of a trained model's weights stored as a .pt file. Like a game save point — allows reusing training without starting from scratch.

01_train_grayscale_vae.ipynb — Training the Base and Sharp VAE

Purpose: Train a VAE on all three datasets. Two variants — "Base" (scheduled beta 0→1, 10 epochs) and "Sharp" (fixed beta 0.05, 12 epochs for better visual quality).

# Core training loop — repeats for MNIST, FASHION, EMNIST
for ds in ["mnist", "fashion", "emnist"]:
    model = ConvVAE(latent_dim=32)          # compress image to 32 numbers
    optimizer = Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
    beta_scheduler = BetaScheduler(start=0.0, end=1.0, n_steps=5000)

    for epoch in range(10):
        for x, _ in loader:
            recon, mu, logvar = model(x)    # encode then decode
            beta = beta_scheduler.step()    # gradually increase KL weight
            loss, _, _ = vae_loss(recon, x, mu, logvar, beta)
            loss.backward()                 # compute gradients
            optimizer.step()               # update weights

# Sharp VAE: same loop but beta = 0.05 (fixed, prioritises clarity)
beta = 0.05  # LOW beta = sharper images, less KL regularisation

02_train_fashion_vae_gan.ipynb — VAE + GAN for Enhanced Sharpness

Purpose: Add a GAN discriminator on top of the VAE. The discriminator is a critic that tells the VAE when its images look fake, forcing progressively better outputs.

# Two competing optimisers
opt_vae  = Adam(vae.parameters(),  lr=1e-3)
opt_disc = Adam(disc.parameters(), lr=1e-4)  # disc learns slower for stability

for x, _ in loader:
    # Step 1: Train Discriminator — learn to tell real from fake
    real_pred = disc(x)
    fake_pred = disc(recon.detach())           # .detach() = stop grad to VAE
    d_loss = bce(real_pred, real_labels) + bce(fake_pred, fake_labels)
    d_loss.backward()

    # Step 2: Train VAE — fool the discriminator
    recon, mu, logvar = vae(x)
    vae_recon_loss, _, _ = vae_loss(recon, x, mu, logvar, beta=0.05)
    gan_loss = bce(disc(recon), real_labels)   # VAE wants disc to say "real"
    total_loss = vae_recon_loss + 0.1 * gan_loss  # lambda_gan=0.1 keeps balance

Analogy: An art student (VAE) whose work is constantly reviewed by a harsh art critic (discriminator). The criticism forces better drawings over time.

Output: vae_fashion_gan.pt

6. Findings — What We Observed

📉
Loss Consistently Decreased — The Model Did LearnAll three datasets showed clear loss reduction across epochs. MNIST (simplest) dropped fastest. EMNIST (most complex, 47 classes) had the highest absolute loss throughout — expected given its scale and variety.
🔪
Low Beta (0.05) Produced Noticeably Sharper ImagesThe Sharp VAE generated more defined, clearer images than the Base VAE. This confirms the beta trade-off: lower beta sacrifices latent organisation for visual clarity — a worthwhile trade for a demonstration project.
⚔️
GAN Training Showed a Healthy Adversarial DynamicGAN discriminator loss rose from ~3,480 to ~8,800 over 10 epochs while VAE loss fell from ~3.5M to ~1.5M. This is the correct GAN pattern — the harder the critic, the better the generator is forced to become. Lambda_gan = 0.1 kept the balance stable.
🎯
CNN Achieved Strong Accuracy — Reliable Evaluator EstablishedMNIST reached 99.26% and FashionMNIST 93.73% after just 8 CPU epochs. When this same CNN later scores generated images, the results are credible and trustworthy.
⚠️
EMNIST Classifier Hit a Configuration ErrorEMNIST has 47 character classes; the classifier was set up for 10. This caused a "Target 26 is out of bounds" error. Fix: change num_classes from 10 to 47. MNIST and FashionMNIST were completely unaffected.
🗺️
Latent Space Became Meaningful and NavigableClass-anchored generation in the app consistently produced recognisable images with CNN confidence of 75–95%. Similar images naturally cluster in the latent space, making targeted, class-specific generation reliable.
🌫️
Some Blurriness Is Expected — Not a FailureVAEs inherently produce slightly blurry images due to probabilistic decoding. The GAN reduces this but cannot eliminate it. This is a fundamental VAE characteristic, acknowledged in the app footer — not a bug.

Overall Summary

The project successfully demonstrates a complete generative AI pipeline. The VAE learned meaningful compressed representations of three image datasets, reducing loss by 50–67% across all training runs. The Sharp training variant (beta = 0.05) and optional GAN boost produced visually clearer outputs. The ResNet-18 classifier, trained on real images with near-perfect accuracy (99.26% MNIST, 93.73% Fashion), provides a trustworthy benchmark for evaluating generated image quality. The Streamlit app unifies everything in a user-facing demo with live image generation, latent exploration, class-anchored synthesis, and Grad-CAM explainability — making the results fully accessible without any code knowledge.
